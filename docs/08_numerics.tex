Among the reference fits, the Neural ODE has the smallest in-sample RMSE. The classical
logistic baseline also fits well with only two physical parameters. Its
Gaussian residual complexity scores are lower than those of the neural
models under the particular parameter-count convention used here.

The deterministic logistic PINN gives $r=\detR$ and $K=\detK$, while the
stochastic fit gives $r=\stoR$, $K=\stoK$, and $\sigma=\stoSigma$.
Both now use the same capacity lower bound as the classical baseline. The
previous PINN constraint placed $K$ above every noisy observation and excluded
the classical optimum; the earlier error gap therefore conflated constraint
choice with loss formulation and optimization. Matching constraints improves
the comparison, but does not establish that a fixed epoch budget reaches the
optimum. Sensitivity checks and longer optimization are separate diagnostics.

The AIC and BIC columns are Gaussian residual complexity scores. Neural SDE
training uses a transition likelihood and PINN training uses a composite
objective, so their fitted weights do not maximize this residual likelihood.
All neural weights are counted, even though the number of weights greatly
exceeds the number of scalar observations. These columns are descriptive
penalties and do not establish a definitive likelihood-based or Bayesian
ranking of the mechanisms.

The Neural SDE simulation uses a maximum normalized-time step of 0.001.
The logistic simulation uses a maximum original-time step of 0.05. These
integration settings are independent of plotting resolution. Refining the
forward solver does not refine the Euler transition approximation used during
training. In particular, the Neural SDE has substantially lower empirical
coverage after integration refinement than in the earlier one-step rollout.
A high coverage value from the coarse rollout did not establish calibration
of its continuous-time interpretation. Both coverage statistics use the
training observations, including fixed initial values, and are conditional on
point estimates. The following sections report the executed forecasting
investigation and the subsequent Bayesian uncertainty extension separately.
